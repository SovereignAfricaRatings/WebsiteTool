    What sits behind this tool

    Public indicators

    Fiscal, external, resource and trade series for 103 sovereigns, with derived ratios and continental aggregates. Shown throughout this tool.

    Multi-pillar scorecard

    Around fifty quantitative and qualitative variables across five analytical pillars, calibrated separately for African and European sovereigns.

    Analyst assessment

    Structured judgement on policy credibility, institutions and event risk, each input documented with its rationale and audit trail.

    Scenario analysis

    Commodity, currency, rate and political shocks run through the full scorecard to test how a sovereign's position would move.

    Risk monitoring

    A continuous feed of market and political events routed to the variables they affect, with a risk register for each sovereign.

    Issuer engagement

    A documented channel for sovereigns to submit evidence and respond before publication, with every change traceable.

    How the figures are built

    Headline fiscal, current-account, growth and inflation figures are three-year averages of the latest actual years; debt and revenue are the latest actual year. IMF projections appear only in trend charts, shaded. Debt to revenue divides gross debt by revenue. Dollar values multiply a published ratio by nominal GDP. Continental figures are ratios of continental totals, so larger economies weigh more; reserve cover is weighted by imports and resource dependence by exports.

    Every live value passes a plausibility check before it is used. Values outside the range are held back and listed in the connector log, and the snapshot value stays in place.

    What is left out, and why

    Euro-area members hold reserves within the euro system, so national reserve cover is shown as not applicable. External debt comes from World Bank debt statistics, which cover low- and middle-income economies only. Where a sovereign does not publish a series, the gap is shown rather than estimated.

    What this tool is not

    Nothing here is a credit rating, a rating outlook or an indication of one, and nothing here is investment advice. AfCRA's ratings are issued only through its formal rating process.
